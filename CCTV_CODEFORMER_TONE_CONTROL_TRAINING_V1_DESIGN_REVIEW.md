# Tone-control training V1: mechanism and next-design review

The genuine 50-update controller failed its preservation, dark-retention and combined known-TRAIN stress requirements. Preserve the trained composite and the earlier full-coarse fine-tuned restorer as research artifacts. Neither is an accepted application model. The next work should resolve the selective-response failure before another training recipe; extending or repeating this controller is unsupported.

The restoration-first goal remains broader than tone. A useful result must preserve visible eyes, expression, mouth, glasses and facial outline on normal inputs while retaining useful restoration on dark and compound inputs. Native CCTV has no invented clean target. Insufficient or covered evidence still requires clearer or less-covered input. The current scalar controller cannot create new geometric detail: its CodeFormer parent remains frozen, and its only output operation is a common RGB shift followed by clipping. Clipping can itself alter chroma, contrast and edge responses. Passing a tone-capacity oracle would not establish facial restoration or identity recovery.

## The failure is already visible during TRAIN

The [saved TRAIN response review](outputs/codeformer_tone_control_training_v1_return_review/state_v1/training_response_review_v1.json) verifies all 200 retained input identities, 400 frozen-parent RAW arrays and 50 attempt/commit histories. Normal targets request a mean change of −0.027941 on average: 93 request a decrease and seven an increase. Dark targets equal their own frozen-base means, within the disclosed CPU64 versus CUDA-FP32 readout resolution. They request zero behavioral change.

Under the 50 changing pre-update heads, normal mean squared error falls from 0.00105208 at zero offset to 0.000492383; 85 normal observations improve, 13 worsen and two remain within readout resolution. For dark observations, 98 of 100 recorded offsets are negative and 98 worsen relative to zero offset, with mean squared error 0.000154585. The combined recorded objective is about 38.5% lower despite this dark leakage. Equal normal/dark sample counts therefore did not enforce preservation. The intended selective-learning hypothesis failed in this run.

This supports a shared-response compromise in the mixed behavioral objective. It does not isolate a unique cause. Every observation was recorded once before its corresponding update, under a changing head; a frozen-final evaluation of all 200 fitted observations was not returned. Objective balance, optimization, feature discrimination, capacity and distribution shift remain competing explanations. Final Adam moments prove neither a per-example gradient history nor the reason for the universal negative output. All 64 support-feature columns were constant one during TRAIN, so partial-support behavior was not learned from these observations. Here support denotes image/letterbox geometry, **not** a covering or completion mask.

The behavioral teacher also matters. Normal targets imitate untouched CodeFormer's global mean, not a clean-tone oracle or a pixel/structure preservation objective. Matching that mean cannot alone guarantee clean MAE, chroma, visible appearance or geometry. The positive retrospective scalar oracle used per-case clean-target information; it established bounded correction capacity for those cases, not a deployable controller signal or successful selective learning.

## One bounded question before selecting another recipe

The primary next question is: **does the frozen final head already fail to separate normal correction from dark preservation on the fitted identities, or does that failure principally appear on identities it never fitted?** This is a finite response measurement, not another optimization attempt, brightness grid or architectural success claim.

1. Freeze the existing initial and final head states and the exact historical full-coarse parent. Reuse the hash-bound input/base caches for all 200 fitted observations. Do not relabel old cached forwards as new calls.
2. Prospectively select 32 approved TRAIN identities absent from the 100 fitted identities and from all DEV/FINAL identities, using canonical source-hash order. Freeze one matched normal/dark camera pair per identity, its source/record/input hashes and one seed/policy before any model call. These 64 observations are an identity-held-out TRAIN measurement; no new fitting occurs.
3. Evaluate both frozen heads on all 264 observations. For the 64 new observations, evaluate untouched-original and full-coarse parents once each to construct the same declared behavioral scalar targets. Return actual RAW/PNG outputs, offsets, clipping, normal-target residuals and dark zero-change residuals for every paired identity. Role labels select audit targets only; they do not enter controller inference.
4. Close an exact maximum census of **528 head forwards + 128 CodeFormer forwards = 656 neural forwards; zero gradient queries, backwards, optimizer constructions or updates**. Preserve 528 head RAW/PNG pairs and all 128 new parent RAW outputs. Use one manual L4 measurement with a 900-second worker cap, 8-GiB RSS, 22-GiB CUDA-reserved cap, 4-GiB disk reserve, at most 1-GiB payload and 3,000 regular files. Stop on any bound or parity failure; no retry or automatic continuation follows.
5. Report continuous paired residuals and loss changes against the exact zero-head baseline for fitted and held-out identities. Retain the present 406-case DEV evidence and every unchanged quality requirement. Do not choose a new gate, rate, weight or model by searching DEV/FINAL outcomes.

The discriminating result is the fixed-state contrast. Persistent final fitted-dark error directly disconfirms a claim that DEV shift alone explains the failure. Selectivity on fitted identities with loss of selectivity on held-out identities would support a generalization/discrimination problem. Failure on both leaves optimization, objective compromise and representation limits unresolved; it does not justify more epochs. Cases requesting positive normal correction are a specific falsifier of a uniformly negative learned response. Report exact errors and identities rather than inventing a new pass threshold.

This measurement is the lowest-risk next discriminator because it uses the actual trained state, exposes the missing fixed-final response, and changes no model. It does not by itself deliver a useful restorer. Before any later genuine training trial, choose a single mechanism that addresses the measured failure: explicit paired normal/dark preservation with an inference-available discriminatory signal, or a restoration objective that protects visible structure as well as tone. Observable disagreement between the untouched and adapted restorers is one candidate signal for a separate cost/causality review; it is not established as the solution. An absolute-input-brightness threshold repeats the earlier overlap failure, and merely freezing the already nearly unchanged upstream route does not address the measured controller leakage.

For the full goal, retain the earlier full-coarse fine-tuned restorer as the strongest audited **research restoration parent**, preserve both normal and dark constraints, and treat scalar tone control as one failed auxiliary experiment. Do not promote the current composite, substitute a clean-target oracle at inference, tune away the failed thresholds, export a base-only fallback as this composite, extend the 50 updates, or launch a new packet from this review. Any later training must return genuine checkpoints, complete same-state DEV controls and all planned visual evidence before acceptance.
