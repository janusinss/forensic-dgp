## Confirmed input workflow

1. Inspect the uploaded original crop and its readable facial structure. Preserve original bytes. Refuse insufficient information or an out-of-scope face; a generated plausible face cannot supply the input admission decision.
2. Estimate a localized blur region and a proposed surroundings blur strength. The first family is localized softness/blur; random grain, JPEG artifacts, privacy mosaics and other families do not receive copied corruption by assumption. Unknown or mixed degradation must allow ordinary R2 restoration or manual setup without an invented automatic match. Gaussian/defocus-like behavior is the first controlled study; motion-specific matching requires separate evidence.
3. Display the proposal with separate editable **Protect** and **Apply blur** areas. Brush/erase, undo, reset and native-coordinate readback are planned. Protect includes the complete existing blur patch and its soft edges. Protect wins when masks overlap. The application mask limits where new blur is allowed; it must not silently expand into excluded pixels. Existing manual rectangle/wild5 functionality remains available while painting is developed.
4. Show the actual derived input, protected-area overlay, estimated strength and original before explicit confirmation. Keep a bypass to ordinary restoration. Recompute the derived image from the immutable original, never from a previously processed preview. Changing the image, mask or strength invalidates the earlier preview and confirmation.
5. Run the selected restoration model only after review. Retain original, protected/application masks, derived input, RAW/PNG outputs and processing provenance in downloads. Automatic proposals and assisted corrections remain separately labelled.

The automatic estimator is a proposal, not a qualified detector or recovered true blur kernel. Smooth skin/background can resemble blur; a globally soft image may have no eligible sharp surroundings. The user may correct or decline a proposal. Exact no-added-blur protection is enforceable inside the final reviewed protection mask; discovering every previously degraded pixel automatically is not guaranteed. If protection covers everything or the application mask is empty, preserve the input and use ordinary restoration rather than add another global blur.

The existing reviewed wild5 transform uses an 8-native-pixel outward guard and a 16-native-pixel outside transition, and its selected native face pixels stay exact. These measured settings are a reference fixture, not proven universal automatic settings. A generalized application mask must keep its feathering within the allowed application area and outside protection. Prepared 256 interpolation boundaries require the existing conservative interior check; exact native protection does not imply every boundary interpolation pixel or generated face remains unchanged. Blur matching remains approximate.

## R2-bound architecture preparation

A separate source-only prototype is now at `research/codeformer_r2_spatial_correction_v1/model.py`. It adapts the preserved original-base spatial-correction design to require the exact R2 canonical state. All 515 R2 tensors stay frozen/eval; 36 new tensors with 168,960 parameters condition a bounded post-SFT correction jointly on encoder, decoder, fused and coarse features. At a site, `z_new = z + tanh(h) * (z - d)`. Zero final projections algebraically start at R2. This differs from the failed encoder-only skip55/U96/S96 route and from updating all stock fusion weights. It is a hypothesis, not demonstrated improvement.

The fixed codebook/token predictor may still limit improvement, and a small stock residual limits this bounded correction. Do not silently unlock other layers or copy a failed recipe after a stop.
