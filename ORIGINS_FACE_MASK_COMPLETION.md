## Abstract and Scope

This document presents a comprehensive, strictly chronological, thesis-style technical history of how the **Face Mask Detection (Covering Proposal Segmentation)** and **Facial Completion (Inpainting)** subsystem was conceived, architected, falsified through iterative empirical trials, mathematically reformulated, and ratified from a zero-prior scratch prototype to the deployed **Trained CodeFormer Completion R2 (`v3`)** production release. ## Chapter 1: Genesis — From Restoration-Only to Coupled Scratch Segmentation and Completion (25 September 2026)

### 1.1 Problem Formulation and Architectural Separation from Visible-Face Restoration

Prior to 25 September 2026, the project's neural pipeline addressed a single inverse problem: **blind facial restoration** of low-resolution, compressed, blurred, and low-light surveillance imagery in which the subject's facial anatomy remained unobscured. However, real-world closed-circuit television (CCTV) and wild forensic photographs frequently exhibit **structural facial occlusions**—surgical and cloth face masks, respirators, dark sunglasses, specular lens glare, hands held over the eyes or mouth, winter scarves, hair strands, and foreground objects. 

On 25 September 2026 ([`FACE_COMPLETION_RESEARCH.md`](FACE_COMPLETION_RESEARCH.md)), the research scope was formally expanded to address single-image facial completion under explicit forensic constraints. A foundational architectural axiom was established at inception: continuing to train a global visible-face restoration network for additional epochs cannot solve structural occlusion removal, because a restorer is trained to sharpen and denoise existing pixel structures rather than replace an opaque foreign object (such as a cloth mask or hand) with missing facial anatomy. Conversely, forcing a single monolithic network to simultaneously restore visible skin and hallucinate occluded regions risks corrupting unoccluded, forensically critical facial traits.

Consequently, the problem was decomposed into three distinct, sequential operations on a single uploaded image without requiring a second reference photograph of the individual:
1. **Visible-Region Restoration:** Enhance degraded visible facial regions while preserving their geometric and photometric structure.
2. **Covering Proposal Segmentation (Mask Detection):** Estimate a binary spatial support map $M \in \{0, 1\}^{H \times W}$ identifying only the foreign covering or occlusion while excluding unoccluded facial skin, transparent eyeglass frames, and ordinary cranial hair.
3. **Support-Restricted Facial Completion:** Synthesize a single plausible anatomical estimate strictly inside the reviewed removal support $M = 1$, while copying every visible pixel outside the support ($M = 0$) identically from the prepared or restored canvas.

### 1.2 The Coupled Custom Scratch Baseline (`GatedUNet` Pilot)

To establish a controllable baseline with zero external model dependencies ([`COMPLETION_TRAINING.md`](COMPLETION_TRAINING.md)), a two-module custom architecture was implemented from scratch in [`completion.py`](completion.py) and [`completion_data.py`](completion_data.py):
- **Covering Proposal Segmenter (`GatedUNet(3, 1, 24)`):** A lightweight 50-tensor gated U-Net accepting a 3-channel RGB image normalized to $[0, 1]$ at $256 \times 256$ resolution with base channel width $24$, predicting a single-channel covering probability map via sigmoid activation.
- **Completion Generator (`GatedUNet(4, 3, 48)`):** A gated U-Net accepting a 4-channel tensor comprising the masked RGB canvas (where pixels inside $M=1$ were replaced with neutral gray $0.5$) concatenated with the binary mask channel $M$, outputting a 3-channel completed RGB image.

Deterministic synthetic training pairs were procedurally rendered from clean face crops by overlaying synthetic occluders across four geometric families: lower-face masks (`lower`), periocular bands (`eyes`), arbitrary rectangular/elliptical objects (`object`), and irregular polygons (`irregular`), alongside unoccluded negative controls (`uncovered`, $20\%$ of samples). A two-epoch joint pilot run (`completion_pilot/epoch_2.pth`, SHA-256 `c2d4cd180226413af63bcfc2a3e17461cfa95f87aff9a8998b893fc3afc42e93`) was executed on a cloud GPU VM.

### 1.3 Why the Scratch Generator Failed and Why `mask-only-v1` Compositing Was Enforced

Empirical and visual evaluation of `completion_pilot/epoch_2.pth` revealed two decisive findings that shaped the remainder of the project:
1. **Capacity Deficit of Scratch Convolutional Inpainting:** While the lightweight `GatedUNet(4, 3, 48)` learned to interpolate smooth skin-toned gradients across synthetic holes, it lacked a high-capacity facial structural prior. Across large lower-face or periocular occlusions, it produced featureless, blurry colour patches rather than coherent mouths, noses, or eyes.
2. **Boundary Contamination from External Feathering:** The initial compositing pipeline applied a 3-pixel external Gaussian feather band outside the binary mask boundary to soften transition seams. Quantitative auditing demonstrated that external feathering altered unoccluded visible pixels outside $M = 1$, violating the strict visible-preservation invariant even when the user supplied an exact oracle mask. In response, the compositing policy was permanently locked to **`mask-only-v1`** (blending radius $0$ outside support), guaranteeing that every pixel outside the confirmed binary mask is copied bit-for-bit from the input canvas.

## Chapter 2: The Pretrained CodeFormer Prior Pivot and the Detector Generalization Crisis (26 – 30 September 2026)

### 2.1 Pivot to the Pretrained S-Lab CodeFormer 512-Codebook Inpainting Prior (26 September 2026)

Recognizing that convincing synthesis of missing eyes, noses, and mouths from a single degraded image requires a learned discrete facial codebook prior trained on millions of facial exemplars, the scratch `GatedUNet(4, 3, 48)` generator was retired on 26 September 2026 ([`PRETRAINED_COMPLETION.md`](PRETRAINED_COMPLETION.md)). In its place, the official **S-Lab CodeFormer Inpainting** architecture (`CodeFormer(dim_embd=512, n_head=8, n_layers=9, codebook_size=512, connect_list=['32', '64', '128'])`, upstream source revision `b33cc7d639d6545bfcccc7e0bc6ae51f24e79c2b`, pretrained checkpoint `codeformer_inpainting.pth`, SHA-256 `b0d1b868c3dacf75d637bbcc0d4b3dd4ce2a064a338dfb4d35c740d3b4ae8797`) was adapted in [`pretrained_completion.py`](pretrained_completion.py).

Unlike the visible-face restoration variant of CodeFormer (which uses a 1024-entry codebook and modulates decoder features via controllable fidelity weights $w \in [0, 1]$), the dedicated CodeFormer inpainting model operates on a 512-entry VQGAN discrete codebook with a 9-layer Transformer predictor and three multi-scale encoder-to-decoder feature-fusion modules (`fuse_convs_dict` at $32 \times 32$, $64 \times 64$, and $128 \times 128$). In the adapted inference pipeline:
- The $256 \times 256$ input canvas and binary removal mask $M$ are upsampled to $512 \times 512$ (bilinear for RGB, nearest-neighbor for $M$).
- Pixels inside the masked support are white-filled ($+1.0$ in normalized $[-1, 1]$ space, matching upstream CodeFormer inpainting training conventions) so that zero original covering texture leaks into the encoder inside $M=1$.
- The network executes in deterministic evaluation mode (`w=1.0, adain=False`), downsamples the raw float output back to $256 \times 256$, and applies exact `mask-only-v1` composition so that $100\%$ of pixels where $M=0$ remain identical to the input canvas.

At the same milestone, the 50-tensor `GatedUNet(3, 1, 24)` segmenter weights from `completion_pilot/epoch_2.pth` were extracted into a standalone portable checkpoint (`covering_detector_segmenter_v1.pth`, `2,681,549` bytes, SHA-256 `ccc22d524073368bc4f5d6b6d2136c90d0e2a117083e4c75fa98d42d7bfff6cf`) to serve as an optional automatic mask proposal generator in [`completion_web.py`](completion_web.py).

### 2.2 The Synthetic-to-Real Detector Gap and the 4-Image Overfit Diagnostic (27 – 28 September 2026)

While `covering_detector_segmenter_v1.pth` achieved a strong synthetic validation IoU of $0.97469$ on the 400-case procedural benchmark (with $0/80$ false-positive masks on uncovered synthetic controls), evaluating it on a newly curated 100-image real-world covered/uncovered photographic dataset (`dataset/detector_expanded_review`, 68 training, 25 validation, 7 test images) revealed a severe domain gap: initial real validation IoU was only $0.06040$, missing over $93\%$ of real covered pixels ([`DETECTOR_REAL_MASK_TRAINING.md`](DETECTOR_REAL_MASK_TRAINING.md)).

Because the segmenter had only ever seen flat-coloured and Gaussian-noise procedural shapes during the 2-epoch pilot, it failed to recognize pleated surgical masks, patterned cloth masks, dark respirators, and hands. Before launching large-scale retraining, two diagnostic audits were executed on 28 September 2026:
1. **Four-Image Overfit Learnability Test ([`DETECTOR_OVERFIT_RESULTS.md`](DETECTOR_OVERFIT_RESULTS.md)):** To verify that the compact 24-channel `GatedUNet(3, 1, 24)` architecture was not structurally incapable of representing real masks, it was trained for 80 AdamW steps on four real training images (`dev_003` patterned mask, `dev_004` dark respirator, `new_covered_52` pleated mask, and `uncovered_00` negative control) using Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) + soft Dice + a $0.25$-weighted hard-visible negative penalty on the hardest $10\%$ of uncovered pixels. At learning rate $10^{-4}$, mask IoU rose from $0.17920$ to $0.98975$ with $0.0046\%$ visible false positives and $0/1$ negative-control false alarms, proving that the architecture possessed sufficient representational capacity to fit real coverings.
2. **Polygon Label Audit and Manifest V2 ([`DETECTOR_LABEL_AUDIT.md`](DETECTOR_LABEL_AUDIT.md), [`DETECTOR_LABEL_V2_RESULTS.md`](DETECTOR_LABEL_V2_RESULTS.md)):** Contact-sheet inspection of all 42 covered training crops and 14 covered validation crops identified boundary approximation defects in `new_covered_03.png` and documented a non-human mannequin case (`new_covered_40.png`). A refined versioned dataset (`detector_expanded_review_v2`, manifest SHA-256 `e0a28146f1f271b94e26351cc093446ca3ea6e6a843a4c53155aa81382e38180`) corrected the polygon boundaries and reported the mannequin separately, confirming that label noise accounted for less than $0.01$ IoU difference and did not explain the $>60\%$ missed coverage on real masks.

### 2.3 Real-Only, Mixed Replay, Teacher Consistency, and Gradient/Probability Audits (28 – 29 September 2026)

A systematic series of controlled detector training experiments was then executed from `completion_pilot/epoch_2.pth` to test whether fine-tuning on the 68 real training images could improve real-mask recall without violating the strict **synthetic retention gate** (which required maintaining the initialization synthetic IoU of $0.97469$ and $0/80$ uncovered false-positive masks):
- **Real-Only Fine-Tuning ([`DETECTOR_REPLAY_TRAINING.md`](DETECTOR_REPLAY_TRAINING.md), [`DETECTOR_REAL_ONLY_COMPARISON_RESULTS.md`](DETECTOR_REAL_ONLY_COMPARISON_RESULTS.md)):** Training solely on the 68 real images (epoch 9 candidate `balanced_penalty_ep9`) raised real validation IoU from $0.06078$ to $0.33786$, but caused catastrophic forgetting on synthetic retention (synthetic IoU dropped to $0.91828$, with false-positive masks appearing on $9/80$ uncovered synthetic faces). At learning rate $10^{-4}$ without replay, synthetic IoU collapsed further to $0.60377$.
- **Mixed Real + Synthetic Replay and Teacher Consistency ([`DETECTOR_LR_COMPARISON_RESULTS.md`](DETECTOR_LR_COMPARISON_RESULTS.md), [`DETECTOR_PENALTY_COMPARISON_RESULTS.md`](DETECTOR_PENALTY_COMPARISON_RESULTS.md)):** Doubling batch size to 8 (2 real covered, 2 real uncovered, 2 synthetic covered, 2 synthetic uncovered) across 200 hash-deduplicated replay sources and adding a weight-$1.0$ Bernoulli KL divergence consistency loss toward the frozen epoch-2 teacher (`replay_ep10` and `consistency_ep10`) preserved synthetic IoU much better ($0.96869–0.97244$ at learning rate $10^{-5}$), but still missed $62.86\%–77.83\%$ of real covered pixels and produced $1/80$ to $4/80$ false masks on uncovered controls.
- **Loss Gradient and Threshold Probability Audits ([`DETECTOR_LOSS_AUDIT_RESULTS.md`](DETECTOR_LOSS_AUDIT_RESULTS.md), [`DETECTOR_PROBABILITY_AUDIT_RESULTS.md`](DETECTOR_PROBABILITY_AUDIT_RESULTS.md)):** Measuring exact parameter gradients across six balanced batches revealed that the $0.25$ hard-visible background penalty opposed the BCE+Dice covering gradient in $4/6$ batches (median cosine similarity $-0.186$), suppressing interior mask confidence so severely that median predicted probabilities on true covered pixels remained at $1.51 \times 10^{-7}$ initially and only $0.253$ in `consistency_ep10`. Sweeping 99 scalar thresholds from $0.01$ to $0.99$ proved that no scalar threshold could simultaneously satisfy coverage recall and uncovered specificity.

### 2.4 Pretrained Boundary Refinement (`SAM2.1`) and Frozen-Feature Head Termination (29 – 30 September 2026)

To test whether a foundation segmentation model could refine fragmented detector proposals into complete object masks without ground-truth prompts, an experimental adapter ([`SAM2_DETECTOR_BENCHMARK.md`](SAM2_DETECTOR_BENCHMARK.md)) coupled `consistency_ep10` with official **Meta SAM2.1 Tiny** (`sam2.1_hiera_tiny.pt`, SHA-256 `7402e0d864fa82708a20fbd15bc84245c2f26dff0eb43a4b5b93452deb34be69`), seeding SAM2 with the deepest interior pixel and a $10\%$-expanded bounding box of the largest connected detector component. While SAM2 snapped cleanly to high-contrast respirator boundaries, it was class-agnostic: whenever the coarse detector misfired on dark cranial hair, eyeglass frames, or background shadows, SAM2 segmented the entire hair mass or background region with high confidence.

Six subsequent frozen-feature head variants (`Expanded fixed`, `Expanded anatomical`, `Extra-update control`, `Border weight 2`, `Semantic residual`, and `RGB residual`) were audited in [`DETECTOR_ARCHITECTURE_DECISION.md`](DETECTOR_ARCHITECTURE_DECISION.md) and [`DETECTOR_PRETRAINING_REVIEW.md`](DETECTOR_PRETRAINING_REVIEW.md) on 30 September 2026. Even under an idealized oracle presence gate that zeroed out all predictions on known clear faces, their synthetic gated IoUs ($0.93899–0.96284$) fell short of the $0.97469$ baseline. The project formally terminated repetitive frozen-head sweeps in favour of source-qualified occlusion data and pretrained encoder evaluations.

## Chapter 3: Source-Qualified Data, Reflective Detectors, Varied Coverings, and Pipeline Ordering (1 – 5 October 2026)

### 3.1 Aspect-Preserving Source-Qualified Data and Reflective ResNet18 Direct Detection (1 – 2 October 2026)

On 1 October 2026 ([`QUALIFIED_COMPLETION_DATA.md`](QUALIFIED_COMPLETION_DATA.md)), [`completion_data_v2.py`](completion_data_v2.py) introduced strict cryptographic and anatomical admission controls for completion and segmentation data: every training source required verified byte hashes, human-reviewed eye coordinates, explicit uncovered-reference certification, and uniform aspect-preserving square padding with a binary `valid` mask excluding padded borders from loss reductions.

Using a 28-source, 280-case reflection and occlusion pilot, a higher-capacity **ResNet18 U-Net** direct covering detector was trained on the cloud VM through epoch 42 and evaluated on 2 October 2026 ([`PRACTICAL_DIRECT_DETECTOR_RESULTS.md`](PRACTICAL_DIRECT_DETECTOR_RESULTS.md)) across 36 native and synthetically degraded photographic views (10 practical cohort sources + 8 RealOcc sources). Although the reflective epoch-42 ResNet18 U-Net detected substantially more native coverings than the retained baseline, its recall collapsed under synthetic CCTV blur and noise, it missed obstructing hair and both nearly hidden hand-over-face cases, and it failed the synthetic retention gate.

### 3.2 Varied Coverings (COFW Cohort), Context Margins, and the Restoration-Before-Completion Ordering Law (3 – 5 October 2026)

Between 3 and 5 October 2026, three critical experiments established the interaction rules between restoration, mask boundaries, and completion:
1. **Varied-Covering Pilot ([`VARIED_COVERING_RESULTS.md`](VARIED_COVERING_RESULTS.md), [`VARIED_COVERING_RESULT_REVIEW.md`](VARIED_COVERING_RESULT_REVIEW.md)):** Incorporating reviewed Caltech Occluded Faces in the Wild (COFW) examples improved exposed training metrics across five covering groups and improved practical hand/scarf proposals, but failed retention and complete-hair-detection checks across the 1,653-member return archive (`varied-covering-results.tar.gz`). Visual inspection of 20 reconstructed faces showed that while central coverings were removed, residual covering fringes left outside an incomplete mask caused the CodeFormer generator to propagate scarf wool or sunglasses rims back into the hole.
2. **Conditioning Context Margin Trial ([`COMPLETION_CONTEXT_RESULTS.md`](COMPLETION_CONTEXT_RESULTS.md)):** Expanding the internal conditioning mask by a narrow margin reduced wool texture contamination on a difficult knit-scarf case, but risked erasing adjacent visible features on tight periocular masks.
3. **DGP App V3 Covering Review — Why Restoration Must Precede Completion ([`CCTV_DGP_APP_V3_COVERING_RESULTS.md`](CCTV_DGP_APP_V3_COVERING_RESULTS.md)):** On 5 October 2026, a 36-case full-pipeline evaluation tested whether running the trained DGP visible-face restorer *after* facial completion improved overall image coherence. The experiment proved the opposite: applying global restoration after completion softened visible identity traits and blurred the exact visible-pixel preservation boundary. This established the immutable **pipeline ordering law**: visible-face restoration (`Conditioned SFT V2`) must execute **first** on the uploaded image, human-reviewed mask proposal/editing must operate **second** on the restored $256 \times 256$ canvas, and facial completion must execute **last** strictly inside the confirmed mask support with zero post-completion global filtering.

## Chapter 4: Alternative Priors (`MAT`, `MI-GAN`) and Surgical Inference-Time Ablations (7 – 24 October 2026)

### 4.1 Benchmarking External Pretrained Face Inpainting Priors: `MAT` and `MI-GAN` (7 – 24 October 2026)

Before committing to fine-tuning the 512-codebook CodeFormer inpainting network on cloud GPUs, two prominent external pretrained face-inpainting architectures were benchmarked on the exact same 32 eligible development views (spanning all seven covering families—surgical/cloth masks, sunglasses, specular glare, hands over eyes/mouth, obstructing hair, winter scarves, and foreground flowers/leaves—plus clear-glasses and uncovered controls):
1. **Mask-Aware Transformer (`MAT` FFHQ-512, [`CCTV_DGP_COMPLETION_MAT_SOURCE_REVIEW_V1.md`](CCTV_DGP_COMPLETION_MAT_SOURCE_REVIEW_V1.md), [`CCTV_DGP_COMPLETION_MAT_MIRROR_V1_RESULTS.md`](CCTV_DGP_COMPLETION_MAT_MIRROR_V1_RESULTS.md)):** Evaluated using the pinned third-party FP16 EMA conversion (`spacepxl/MAT-inpainting-fp16`, revision `bef6e8b7d535abe2d42fdaeafec903abdfc638a3`). While MAT generated plausible isolated eyes and mouths on clean crops, it left conspicuous hand-to-cheek seams, failed on strong eyeglass glare, and hallucinated jarring high-frequency textures on degraded surveillance inputs.
2. **MI-GAN 256 (`completion_migan256_comparison_v1`, [`scripts/run_completion_migan256_v1.py`](scripts/run_completion_migan256_v1.py)):** Evaluated at native $256 \times 256$ resolution. Although lightweight and fast on CPU, MI-GAN exhibited severe structural distortion and blurry anatomical collapse across large lower-face and periocular masks compared to CodeFormer's discrete VQGAN codebook prior.

### 4.2 Localizing the Root Causes of CodeFormer Inpainting Defects (7 – 14 October 2026)

Why did the pretrained S-Lab CodeFormer inpainting model (`codeformer_inpainting.pth`) still exhibit residual eyewear outlines, asymmetric gaze, false chroma tinting on grayscale CCTV inputs, and boundary seams across the 32 development cases? Five surgical, zero-training diagnostic experiments isolated the exact mechanisms:
1. **Input Footprint Tightening ([`CCTV_DGP_COMPLETION_INPUT_FOOTPRINTS_V1_RESULTS.md`](CCTV_DGP_COMPLETION_INPUT_FOOTPRINTS_V1_RESULTS.md), 7 October 2026):** Tracing tighter input-only object footprints preserved more surrounding skin but left subtle sub-pixel shadow/rim cues outside the mask, prompting CodeFormer to regenerate dark sunglasses frames and hair strands inside the hole.
2. **Saved-Pixel Support Localization ([`CCTV_DGP_COMPLETION_PIXEL_SUPPORT_V1_RESULTS.md`](CCTV_DGP_COMPLETION_PIXEL_SUPPORT_V1_RESULTS.md), 7 October 2026):** Auditing eight diagnostic support windows proved that observed completion defects arose from two distinct mechanisms: (a) **copied exterior remnants** outside the reviewed mask (which no generator can alter under exact outside-mask preservation), and (b) **internal generative defects** inside the mask (such as clouded glare replacements, mouth expression drift, and chromatic false colour on monochrome inputs).
3. **Conditioning Union vs. Margin Feathering ([`CCTV_DGP_COMPLETION_CONDITIONING_UNION_V1_RESULTS.md`](CCTV_DGP_COMPLETION_CONDITIONING_UNION_V1_RESULTS.md), [`CCTV_DGP_COMPLETION_MARGIN_FEATHER_V1_RESULTS.md`](CCTV_DGP_COMPLETION_MARGIN_FEATHER_V1_RESULTS.md), 8 October 2026):** Masking the union of broader context during encoder conditioning while compositing only the core mask reduced regenerated glasses and flower petals, and linear Euclidean 2-pixel internal margin feathering ($1 - d/3$) softened outer-edge jumps across all 28 non-empty cases, yet central anatomical and chromatic defects persisted.
4. **Disabling Encoder-to-Decoder Feature Fusion (`w=0`, [`CCTV_DGP_COMPLETION_FEATURE_FUSION_OFF_V1_RESULTS.md`](CCTV_DGP_COMPLETION_FEATURE_FUSION_OFF_V1_RESULTS.md), 10 October 2026):** In CodeFormer's architecture, the Transformer predicts discrete $16 \times 16$ codebook indices *before* the three `fuse_convs_dict` blocks (`32`, `64`, `128`) add skip-connected encoder features into the VQGAN decoder. Setting $w=0$ bypassed all three fusion blocks while keeping the predicted codebook indices identical. Visual inspection of all 32 delivered images at $w=0$ revealed severe regressions—bright red patches on lower faces and loss of surrounding lighting/skin-tone continuity—proving that `fuse_convs_dict` is indispensable for anchoring the decoded face to the visible context, and that **model weights themselves had to be adapted via gradient-based fine-tuning**.
5. **Detector Confidence Diagnostic and Native Mask Editor Fidelity Repair ([`CCTV_DGP_DETECTOR_CONFIDENCE_V1_R1_RESULTS.md`](CCTV_DGP_DETECTOR_CONFIDENCE_V1_R1_RESULTS.md), [`CCTV_DGP_NATIVE_MASK_EDITOR_V1_RESULTS.md`](CCTV_DGP_NATIVE_MASK_EDITOR_V1_RESULTS.md), 14 October 2026):** A frozen 36-view CPU confidence diagnostic confirmed that `covering_detector_segmenter_v1.pth` produced empty proposals on $4/14$ original covered photos and $11/14$ synthetically degraded covered views while misfiring on an uncovered control ($11,683$ pixels). Consequently, the human-in-the-loop mask editor in `static/face_workflow.js` was repaired to preserve exact native mask resolution without downscaling distortion (verified across $1024 \times 768$, $1280 \times 720$, and $4096 \times 3906$ fixtures with zero altered pixels).

## Chapter 5: Workspace Isolation, Gradient Verification, and Trained CodeFormer Completion R1 (25 – 28 October 2026)

### 5.1 Branch Isolation, Manual Prototype, and the HQ512 TRAIN Baseline Probe (25 October 2026)

On 25 October 2026 ([`FACE_MASK_COMPLETION_SPEC_V1.md`](FACE_MASK_COMPLETION_SPEC_V1.md), [`WORKSPACE_STATUS.md`](WORKSPACE_STATUS.md)), the repository was split into two physically isolated Git worktrees so that visible-face restoration (`RESTORATION` in `C:\xampp\htdocs\YEAR 4\Testing_RESTORATION`) and covering detection/completion (`FACE_MASK_COMPLETION` in `C:\xampp\htdocs\YEAR 4\Testing_FACE_MASK_COMPLETION`) could evolve concurrently with zero file contention.

A hash-admitted local manual-mask CodeFormer prototype ([`COMPLETION_CODEFORMER_MANUAL_V1.md`](COMPLETION_CODEFORMER_MANUAL_V1.md)) was launched alongside a 30-request paired calibration probe on six high-resolution `TRAIN` photographs across four canonical occlusion geometries (`lower_face`, `eye_band`, `single_lens`, `cheek_object`) plus empty controls ([`COMPLETION_CODEFORMER_HQ_TRAIN_PROBE_V1_RESULTS.md`](COMPLETION_CODEFORMER_HQ_TRAIN_PROBE_V1_RESULTS.md)). Baseline pretrained CodeFormer achieved a paired masked MAE of $0.0537915$ and boundary-gradient error of $0.0222224$, providing the quantitative anchor for fine-tuning.

### 5.2 Parameter-Group Partitioning and the L4 Gradient Diagnostic (`25 October 2026`)

Rather than unfreezing the entire 497-tensor CodeFormer network—which would corrupt the pretrained VQGAN codebook (`quantize.embedding`, 1 tensor) and decoder generator (`generator.*` and `post_quant_conv.*`, 164 tensors) on a modest forensic training set—the 497 state-dict tensors were partitioned into **165 frozen generative prior tensors** and **332 trainable adaptation tensors** ($58,459,840$ parameters):
- **Trainable Code-Prediction Path (`278` tensors):** The encoder (`encoder.*`), latent projection (`quant_conv.*`), positional embeddings (`position_emb`), and 9-layer CodeFormer Transformer (`ft_layers.*`, `idx_pred_layer.*`), which map the white-masked $512 \times 512$ input canvas to $16 \times 16 = 256$ discrete codebook logits.
- **Trainable Multi-Scale Feature-Fusion Path (`54` tensors):** The three convolutional fusion modules (`fuse_convs_dict.32`, `fuse_convs_dict.64`, `fuse_convs_dict.128`), which blend unoccluded encoder features into the VQGAN decoder at $32 \times 32$, $64 \times 64$, and $128 \times 128$ scales.

Before running a single optimizer step, a zero-update **Gradient Diagnostic V1 R1** ([`COMPLETION_CODEFORMER_GRADIENT_DIAGNOSTIC_V1.md`](COMPLETION_CODEFORMER_GRADIENT_DIAGNOSTIC_V1.md), [`COMPLETION_CODEFORMER_GRADIENT_V1_RETURN_REVIEW.md`](COMPLETION_CODEFORMER_GRADIENT_V1_RETURN_REVIEW.md)) was executed on the Google Cloud NVIDIA L4 GPU (`g2-standard-4`, return archive SHA-256 `a39f6e0323734a8b13150eadba64d047c240a9bab7186a4bf7f52449d182f73c`). Across 8 fixed `TRAIN` cases and 2,656 audited tensor receipts, it proved that:
1. Cross-entropy and latent MSE losses on the $16 \times 16$ bottleneck produced finite, non-zero gradients across all five code-path parameter groups while leaving the 54 fusion tensors untouched.
2. Pixel-space L1 losses on the decoded RGB output produced finite, non-zero gradients across all three fusion-resolution groups (`32`, `64`, `128`) and the encoder/quantizer path, while straight-through argmax codebook lookup blocked pixel gradients from updating the Transformer `ft_layers` and `idx_pred_layer` directly.

This diagnostic discovery proved that effective CodeFormer inpainting adaptation requires **dual-path supervision**: simultaneous code/latent supervision for the Transformer predictor and decoded RGB supervision for the encoder and multi-scale fusion blocks.

### 5.3 Fine-Tuning V1 Pre-Flight Stop and Trained CodeFormer Completion R1 (`25 – 28 October 2026`)

The initial **Fine-Tuning V1** packet ([`COMPLETION_CODEFORMER_FINETUNE_V1_RETURN_REVIEW.md`](COMPLETION_CODEFORMER_FINETUNE_V1_RETURN_REVIEW.md), return archive SHA-256 `7d51d190d7cba45f6c0f2f99e90e32d06fb5ef43444bf55b07e973ba0ba91bd6`) halted safely at pre-flight (`0` backward calls, `0` optimizer updates) when its step-0 parity check detected a mismatch between the trainer's internal input normalization and the local application's $256 \to 512 \to 256$ white-masked pipeline.

## Chapter 6: Post-R1 Architectural and Objective Isolations — Why Partial Updates Failed (26 – 29 October 2026)

Five rigorous L4 GPU experiments tested isolated hypotheses to overcome these defects—and each falsified a partial-update assumption:

### 6.1 Coverage V2 — Unweighted Expansion and Serialization Timeout (`26 October 2026`)

- **Hypothesis & Execution ([`COMPLETION_CODEFORMER_SAME_CODEBOOK_PROBE_V1_RESULTS.md`](COMPLETION_CODEFORMER_SAME_CODEBOOK_PROBE_V1_RESULTS.md)):** Did V3 and V4 stall because the frozen 512-entry VQGAN inpainting codebook lacked expressive facial tokens, or because the R1 Transformer predictor was selecting suboptimal indices? ### 6.5 Predictor-Only CE V1 — Why Updating Code Prediction Without Fusion Also Fails (`27 October 2026`)
- **Hypothesis & Execution ([`COMPLETION_CODEFORMER_PREDICTOR_CE_V1_RESULTS.md`](COMPLETION_CODEFORMER_PREDICTOR_CE_V1_RESULTS.md)):** Freeze the encoder (`encoder.*`), quantizer (`quant_conv.*`), and all 54 fusion tensors (`fuse_convs_dict.*`) at R1, and train *only* the 110 Transformer predictor tensors (`position_emb`, `ft_layers.*`, `idx_pred_layer.*`) for 32 Adam updates using support-weighted $16 \times 16$ cross-entropy toward clean teacher codes (`232.36\text{ s}` on L4, terminal state SHA-256 `bc7e1bd5db71ffffc55eff4cc2b4da364aa96b9316c09911f741222e3d03f82`).

## Chapter 7: Unified CodeFormer Completion R2 (`v1 -> v2 -> v3`) Ratification, Detector Audit, and Production Release (29 October – 2 November 2026)

### 7.1 The Unified Co-Adaptation Formulation (`Unified R2`)

Synthesizing the lessons of `V1 R1`, `V2`, `V3`, `V4`, `Same-Codebook Probe V1`, and `Predictor CE V1` ([`COMPLETION_CODEFORMER_UNIFIED_R2_V1_DESIGN.md`](COMPLETION_CODEFORMER_UNIFIED_R2_V1_DESIGN.md)), the **Unified CodeFormer Completion R2** architecture re-coupled all **332 trainable tensors** (`278` code-path + `54` fusion-path) resumed from the verified R1 parent state (`0a6b4ad907c6ead86d8f26d3446666387493cdce81c6adc1ed78a8677d50fadd`) under a two-pass per-batch co-adaptation objective:
1. **Support-Weighted $16 \times 16$ Code and Pre-Quantization Latent Pass (`fit_code`):** Max-pools the $512 \times 512$ binary removal mask to the $16 \times 16$ token grid ($M_{16}$) and weights masked token positions by $w_{\text{masked}} = 4.0$ vs. $w_{\text{visible}} = 0.5$ so gradient updates concentrate on occluded facial anatomy, combining weighted cross-entropy with $0.5 \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}}(z_{\text{feat}}, z_{\text{teacher}})$ so the encoder and `quant_conv` co-adapt smoothly with the 9-layer Transformer.
2. **Delivered $256 + 512$ Pixel, Boundary, Gradient, and Differentiable Chroma Guard Pass (`fit_RGB`):** Evaluates the decoded RGB output through the exact differentiable $512 \to 256$ composite delivery operator:
   $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{RGB}} = 1.0 \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{hole},256} + 0.35 \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{hole},512} + 0.35 \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{ring},256} + 0.20 \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad},256} + \mathbf{1}_{\text{gray}} \cdot 0.25 \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{chroma},256}$$
   where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{chroma},256} = \mathbb{E}_{M_{256}}\left[\frac{|R-G| + |G-B| + |R-B|}{3}\right]$ directly penalizes false chromatic tinting inside the hole whenever the visible context is monochrome ($\mathbf{1}_{\text{gray}} = 1$).

## Chapter 8: Synthesis, Forensic Epistemology, and Summary Tables

### 8.1 Architectural and Epistemological Synthesis

The completed system embodies three interlocking design principles validated by five weeks of empirical falsification:
1. **Strict Modularity Between Restoration and Completion:** Visible-face restoration (**Conditioned SFT V2 `p2_u0224`**) and occluded-region completion (**Trained CodeFormer Completion R2 `v3`**) solve fundamentally different inverse problems. Running restoration *first* recovers clean context cues on visible skin, hair, and unoccluded landmarks; locking pixels outside the confirmed removal mask (`mask-only-v1`) guarantees that facial completion never degrades or alters visible forensic evidence.
2. **Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop Mask Governance:** Because compact segmentation networks trained on procedural or small real cohorts exhibit an unavoidable trade-off between degraded-covering recall and uncovered-face false positives, automatic segmentation (`covering_detector_segmenter_v1.pth`) is bounded strictly to an initial editable proposal. The operator must visually inspect, paint/erase at native resolution, and explicitly confirm both the removal footprint and the sufficiency of visible facial structure before completion can execute.
3. **Epistemic Boundary of Generative Completion:** When an opaque covering (such as a surgical mask, hand, or dark sunglasses lens) completely blocks underlying photons, the true hidden facial features are physically absent from the image. Even with a 512-entry discrete VQGAN facial prior and 332 co-adapted parameters, **facial completion produces one plausible anatomical estimate conditioned on visible context—never a verified reconstruction of hidden identity or biometric proof**. Accordingly, every UI view, model card, and downloadable evidence ZIP separates the original upload, the Conditioned SFT V2 restoration, the confirmed binary removal mask, and the R2 completion estimate.
